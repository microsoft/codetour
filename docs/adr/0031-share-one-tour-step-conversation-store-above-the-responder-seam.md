# Share one Tour Step Conversation store above the responder seam

Every playback surface keeps only the painter of its Tour Step Conversations. The conversation lifecycle — the per-step store, the blocking of a question while its conversation's previous answer is in progress, the follow-up history of completed exchanges, the interruption of an answer in flight with its late completion ignored, the failure messages and the release of the responder's agent resources — lives in a single `vscode`-free module above the `StepChatResponder` seam (`src/agent/stepConversations.ts`), which both the Expanded Description View and the terminal reader use unchanged. The grounded step context stays each surface's own knowledge: the webview captures the displayed buffers, the terminal resolves its read-only step context.

Before this decision the lifecycle was implemented once per surface and drifted: the terminal had no interruption (`q` during an answer closed the whole reader), the history filters differed (the terminal excluded empty answers and knew nothing of interrupted exchanges), and only the extension implemented the ordered failover of ADR 0014. The shared module fixes the rules once; the painters convert the stored raw exchanges into their own rendering — sanitized HTML in the webview, DOM rows in the terminal. One history rule results: every exchange that completed feeds the follow-up question, empty answers included; a failed, interrupted or still-pending exchange never does. The terminal now shows the responder's live activity line (agent messages, streamed reasoning, the provider the failover is trying) as the webview always did.

## Consequences

Interruption works everywhere: in the terminal, `q` or Escape while an answer streams stops that answer and only a second press quits the reader. This extends the failover of ADR 0014 to the terminal reader: `openTerminalChat` builds the same fallback responder over the installed candidates, in the extension order, filtered to the executables found on `PATH`; the panel keeps naming the first installed candidate while the fallback reports the provider actually tried. The read-only client policy of ADR 0013 is untouched — the deepening happens strictly above the seam — and the panel-scoped memory of ADR 0019 keeps its per-step separation, now common to both surfaces. The behavior is tested once at the store's interface, in process with deterministic responders, instead of per surface through a PTY.
